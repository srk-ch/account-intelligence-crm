# Video script: 3 minutes, scene by scene

You do not need to know the code. Everything below says **where to click, what is on screen, what to say, and what conclusion to draw**. Read the "Say" lines almost word for word; they are written to be spoken, about 420 words in total (roughly 2 min 50 s at a calm pace).

Live site: https://account-intelligence-crm.vercel.app

---

## Before you press record (5 minutes)
1. Open the live site in a **fresh private window**. Browser zoom **100 to 110%**, bookmarks bar hidden, other tabs closed, notifications off (Windows: Focus assist).
2. Click through once so it is warm (the first load can be slow). Start on **Eastern Counties** (first circle, top right).
3. Record the **browser window only**, 1920x1080 if you can. Windows: **Win + G** (Xbox Game Bar), or OBS, or Loom. Use a headset mic; a quiet room matters more than anything else.
4. Move the mouse **slowly** and **stop on what you are talking about**. Do not read the page aloud; say the one sentence below, then let the page sit for a second.
5. Do the whole thing **twice**, keep the better take. A calm 2:50 beats a rushed 3:00. If you run over, cut scene 6.

## The one idea behind everything (say it in your head, it sets your tone)
"Anyone can paste a total a customer told them. This tool only counts what a file proves, shows where every number came from, and updates itself."

---

## Scene 1 (0:00 to 0:20): What this is

**On screen:** the top of the page: title "Accounts Overview", the row of circles at top right (one per account, each with a red number), the **"The short version"** card and the black **"Do this first"** card.

**Say:**
"This is Account Intelligence, a CRM for a customer success manager who inherits accounts on a Monday. It answers five questions for each account: what is it worth, who holds the power, what do we do first, what is about to go wrong, and what do we not know. It reads live from the game's data, and every number shows where it came from."

**Point out:** the red number on each circle = how many *high* risks that account has. The left icon rail = the five questions.

**Conclusion to draw:** "In ten seconds, a manager knows the value, the worst risk and the first move."

---

## Scene 2 (0:20 to 0:55): Question 1, "What is the book worth?" (the most important scene)

**Where:** click the **Book** icon on the left rail (or scroll down to "1 What is the book worth?"). First, glance at the two panels under the deal board: **"Where the value sits"** (donut) and **"Health metrics"** (three rings).

### What each metric means, and what conclusion to draw

| Metric (where it is) | What it measures | How to read it | Conclusion for Eastern Counties |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Backed** (centre of the donut, and the "short version" sentence) | Money we can prove from a signed contract, paid invoices, or a won offer | Higher is better; this is the only number we are willing to count | £455,000 is real. We can show the file behind every pound. |
| **Pipeline ceilings** (red slice) | The most a deal *could* be worth ("worth up to"), still not won | Shown, never added to the total | £450,000 of upside exists, but it is not money yet. |
| **Open offers** (grey slice) | Things we have offered that nobody has accepted | Same: shown, not counted | £180,000 is waiting on a yes. |
| **Facts verified** (blue ring) | Of everything we know, how much is confirmed by a document, not just said by a person | Low = we are leaning on hearsay | 3 of 20: most of what we "know" is only told or claimed, so we are careful. |
| **Deals agreed** (yellow ring) | Deals that actually reached agreement | | 1 of 4: only one of the four deals is closed. |
| **Docks flying** (grey ring) | Of the drone docks installed, how many flew in the last 4 weeks | This is the customer's real usage | 18 of 35: about half are used, which is the real argument in the renewal. |

**Say:**
"Here is the key decision. The book counts only what a file proves: a signed order plus offers we actually won. That is £455,000. The red slice is pipeline that is not won yet, and the grey slice is open offers. We show them, but we never add them in. Below, the panel marked **"Set apart: not backed"** shows customer claims. For example, the customer said all four forces are keen and the alliance has the budget. Nothing backs that, and the facts contradict it, so it is set apart and never counted. A weaker tool would just add everything up."

**Conclusion to draw:** "The honest number is smaller than the headline, and I can prove every pound of it."

## Scene 3 (0:55 to 1:15): Question 2, "Who holds the power?"

**Where:** click the **Power** icon (third from the top, two people).

**What is on screen:** one card per deal with boxes: **Decides, Pays, Uses it, Could block, Champion, Waiting on**.

**Say:**
"For each deal I show who decides, who pays, who uses it and who could block it, plus our champion and what the deal is waiting on. A deal that looks healthy can have a decider who is not on our side, and this shows it before it costs us."

**Conclusion to draw:** "Two people can want a deal and still not be the ones who sign. This tells you who to call."

---

## Scene 4 (1:15 to 1:45): Question 3, "What do we do first?"

**Where:** click the **First moves** icon (the branching dots). Then click **Draft request** on the first card.

**What is on screen:** numbered moves. Each shows an owner, a due date, **Worth up to**, and **Evidence** (for example "2 of 3 facts verified"). The black first card is move 1.

**Say:**
"The moves are ranked by deadline first, not by deal size, because a person leaving or a public event cannot move. Each has one owner and a date inside its deadline. Move one is to reply in writing to the inspector about training dates before the 5 December event. I can click Draft request and it writes the message with the owner, the date and the evidence behind it, ready to copy."

**(Click Close.)**

**Conclusion to draw:** "A manager gets a plan, not a list of worries."

---

## Scene 5 (1:45 to 2:10): Question 4, "What is about to go wrong?" and the flights chart

**Where:** click the **Risks** icon (triangle, fourth). (Optional: the **"The next … days"** timeline under the deal board shows the same dates in order.)

**What is on screen:** the **Flights per week** bar chart (dark bars = last 4 weeks) and a table: **Status / What / Turns bad**, worst first. Red on the timeline = a deadline that cannot move.

**How to read the chart:** each bar is one week of total flights. If the dark (recent) bars are shorter than the grey ones, usage is falling. The line above it says how many flights in the last 4 weeks compared with the 4 before, as a percentage.

**Say:**
"Risks are listed worst first with when each turns bad. This chart is real usage: if the recent bars shrink, the customer is drifting away before they say so. The risks also include people going cold, meaning nobody has contacted them for more than 45 days."

**Conclusion to draw:** "Usage tells you a renewal is in trouble months before the customer does."

## Scene 6 (2:10 to 2:35): Proof, and then the live part

**Do this (one click):** click any small id chip under a number (for example in the Book section). A side panel opens.

**Say:**
"Click any source and the receipt opens: the exact fact or file behind the number, marked verified, told, or claim."

**(Close it. Now click the Kestrel circle, then the Brindle Harbour circle, the last two.)**

**Say:**
"Two more accounts were released while I was building. I did not add them by hand. The CRM saw them in the feed, pulled their data and files, and built the same five answers. For Brindle Harbour, the customer says the programme saves four hundred thousand a year. That is one person's estimate that finance has never seen, so it is a claim and not counted, and the first move is to get the contractor's knowledge written down before he leaves."

**Conclusion to draw:** "It keeps working when the situation changes, with no code change from me."

## Scene 7 (2:35 to 3:00): Close

**Do this:** switch to the GitHub repo tab for two seconds (https://github.com/srk-ch/account-intelligence-crm), then back to the CRM page.

**Say:**
"The code is public. It makes one call a minute, keeps the token on the server only, and decides with rules a manager can read, not a model that can invent. I built it with Claude Code under my direction: I set the rules, such as backed value only, and checked every figure against its source. What it does not do yet is score a chance of success, because the data cannot support an honest number. Backed value only, a source on everything, and it stays current by itself."

## If you get stuck: one-line cheat sheet
- **Backed:** proven by a file. **Ceiling:** could be worth, not won. **Claim:** said, not proven. **Verified:** confirmed by a document.
- **Facts verified / Deals agreed / Docks flying:** the three rings = how much we can prove, how much is closed, how much is used.
- **High risk number on a circle:** risks that cannot wait.
- **Why deadlines rank first:** a date cannot move; a deal size can.
- **Why rules, not AI scoring:** a manager can read a rule; a model can invent.

## Do not say / avoid
- Do not say the totals match the game's debrief; they do not on purpose (see the thinking doc).
- Do not claim the note write-back works unless you test it first (it saves to the real game).
- Do not read numbers aloud one after another; say the one that proves your point.
